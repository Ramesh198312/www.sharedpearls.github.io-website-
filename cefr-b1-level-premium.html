Independent Communication

CEFR Level B1 Use English on your own terms

B1 is the level at which English stops being a subject and starts being a tool. You learn to follow clear everyday speech and writing, handle most situations that come up while travelling or working, describe experiences, explain your plans and give reasons for what you think — in connected language, without rehearsing every sentence first.

My learning path

Six stages that take you from consolidating A2 to using English independently. Work through them in order, or dip into the stage that matches what you need now.

  1. 1

    Strengthen foundations

    Revisit the structures you bring from A2 so they become reliable rather than remembered.

    Start with grammar
  2. 2

    Expand communication

    Add the grammar, vocabulary and set phrases that let you say more than the minimum.

    Build vocabulary
  3. 3

    Build independence

    Handle longer texts and longer conversations without needing everything simplified for you.

    Read longer texts
  4. 4

    Communicate in context

    Apply English to work, travel, study, technology and the situations you actually meet.

    See real situations
  5. 5

    Integrate skills

    Combine reading, listening, speaking and writing in single tasks, as real communication does.

    Open the modules
  6. 6

    Demonstrate progress

    Review, check what has settled, and show what you can do in an extended communication task.

    Go to review

Dashboard / B1 / Pronunciation

Pronunciation lab

Improve clarity through sounds, stress, rhythm and connected speech. Being understood depends far more on stress and rhythm than on a perfect accent.

Functional English

What you can do with English, expressed as jobs rather than structures: request, refuse, apologise, suggest, clarify, complain politely. This is where grammar earns its keep.

What you are working towards

Plain-English descriptions of what B1 looks like in practice. These are our paraphrases of the CEFR descriptors, not official wording.

Follow the main points of clear, standard speech and writing on familiar matters — work, study, leisure, everyday life.

Deal with most situations that come up while travelling where the language is spoken.

Produce connected language on topics that are familiar or of personal interest, rather than isolated sentences.

Describe experiences, events and ambitions, and tell a story that a listener can follow.

Explain your plans and intentions, and say briefly why you hold a particular view.

Give opinions and reasons for them, and respond to someone who disagrees.

Take a more independent part in everyday interaction — asking, checking, clarifying and repairing misunderstandings.

Write straightforward connected text on familiar subjects, including personal letters and emails.
