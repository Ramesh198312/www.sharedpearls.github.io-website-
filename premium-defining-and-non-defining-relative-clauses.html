01 CORE DISTINCTION & PUNCTUATION

Identify, or add information?

Every relative clause does one of two jobs. Defining clauses narrow the noun down to the one(s) we mean. Non-defining clauses comment on a noun the listener can already identify. The difference is meaning first; punctuation and intonation are how we signal it.

DEFINING · RESTRICTIVE · IDENTIFYING

IDENTIFIES

NOUN↓necessary information↓specific reference

The students who passed the test received certificates.

Answers: Which students? — only the ones who passed. Others failed and got nothing.

NON-DEFINING · NON-RESTRICTIVE

ADDS INFORMATION

already identified noun↓additional information↓“by the way …”

The students, who passed the test, received certificates.

Answers: Tell me more about the students. — all of them passed, all of them got certificates.

The comma switch

The students, who passed the test, received certificates.

03 POSSESSIVE RELATIVES

Whose, of which, with

Possession can be carried by whose for people and things, by the more formal of which patterns for things, or — very often in conversation — by a simple with-phrase.

the company→whose HQ …
the company→the HQ of which …
the man→with the …

06 SENTENTIAL & FUSED RELATIVES

When the antecedent is a whole idea

Which can point back to an entire proposition. And words like whatever and whoever fuse the noun and the relative into one form.

noun←which
whole clause←, which
whatever=anything that

What does which point to?

Same pronoun, two different antecedents.

WHICH = NOUN ANTECEDENT

Joe got the job, which paid well.

which = the job. Replace it with it: “The job paid well.”

WHICH = ENTIRE PRECEDING PROPOSITION

Joe got the job, which surprised everybody.

which = the fact that Joe got the job. Replace it with this: “Joe got the job. This surprised everybody.”

— FINAL SUMMARY

The whole system on one screen

Meaning first

Defining = which one? Non-defining = tell me more. Context decides; punctuation and intonation signal it.

Marking it

Non-defining: two commas mid-sentence, comma + full stop at the end; dashes or brackets possible; two short pauses in speech.

Pronoun choice

who people · which things · that defining only · zero for defining objects · whom formal objects.

Possession & prepositions

whose for people and things; of which formal; fronted in which / with whom formal, stranded (that) … in informal.

Quantity & ideas

some of whom, none of which in non-defining clauses; sentential which (never what) for whole propositions.

Reduction & register

-ing = active, -ed = passive; label informal, non-standard and archaic patterns rather than calling them simply “wrong”.
