What you will be able to do

Learning objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to choose articles for a reason, not by guesswork.

  1. Explain what articles are, where they sit in a noun phrase, and how noun type limits the choice.
  2. Apply the core decision question, 'Does my listener know which one?', to choose a/an, the or Ø.
  3. Choose a or an by sound, and pronounce a, an and the in weak and stressed forms.
  4. Distinguish general, generic and particular reference, including 'half-general' ideas and groups.
  5. Handle institutions, place names, publications, titles, illnesses, body parts and fixed expressions.
  6. Recognise article omission in structures (possessive 's, noun modifiers) and abbreviated styles.
  7. Use possessive determiners and this/that/it correctly alongside articles.
  8. Diagnose and correct typical learner errors and write accurate formal and academic English.

Articles at a glance

A definition

Articles are determiners that open a noun phrase and signal whether the listener can identify what we mean. English has a definite article, the; an indefinite article with two forms, a/an; and a meaningful absence, the zero article Ø.

Article choice depends on four things working together: meaning, shared knowledge between speaker and listener, context, and whether the noun is singular countable, plural or uncountable. Changing the article changes the meaning: I'm going to the bank (you know which) is not the same as Is there a bank near here? (any bank).

A
I saw a fox in the garden.
AN
She is an engineer.
THE
The fox was sleeping by the gate.
Zero (Ø)
Ø Foxes are clever animals.

The core decision

Swan reduces most choices to one question. Ask it every time you use a noun.
