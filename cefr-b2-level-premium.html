Confident & independent communication

CEFR Level B2 · Upper intermediate Say exactly what you mean, at length

B2 is where English becomes precise. You move from getting your point across to shaping it: developing an argument with evidence, weighing one view against another, reading between the lines of a complex text, following fast unscripted discussion, and adjusting your tone for a client, a colleague, a seminar or a friend. This dashboard is the control centre for that work — the range, precision and fluency to communicate effectively across professional, academic and real-world contexts.

    Explore by skill

    Seven language areas, each with its own hub further down the dashboard.

    Grammar & structure

    The B2 grammatical range: tense and aspect across long stretches of text, graded modality, hypothetical worlds, information structure, and the sentence-building that extended writing and speech depend on.

    Vocabulary & lexical power

    At B2, vocabulary stops being a list of words. It becomes collocation, word family, register and connotation — choosing the word that carries exactly the meaning and the tone you intend.

    Reading lab

    Reading at B2 is critical reading: following an argument, separating claim from evidence, hearing the writer's attitude behind the sentences, and comparing how two texts treat the same subject.

    Listening lab

    Extended, unscripted speech: lectures, meetings, debates and interviews at natural speed, with the signposting, attitude and implication that go with them.

    Audio is published with each listening page, together with its transcript and tasks.

    Speaking studio

    Longer turns and real interaction: developing a point, conceding ground without losing it, taking and holding the floor, presenting to an audience, and responding on the spot.

    Writing studio

    Detailed, connected texts with a shape a reader can follow — and the editing habits that turn a first draft into something worth sending.

    Pronunciation lab

    Clarity rather than accent. Stress, rhythm and intonation carry meaning in English — at B2 they become tools you use deliberately, not features you hope come out right.

    Functional language & discourse

    What you do with language rather than what it is made of: disagreeing without damage, hedging a claim, repairing a sentence mid-flow, and keeping a long contribution coherent.

    Professional English

    The workplace half of B2: the meeting you have to contribute to, the email that has to land correctly, the update nobody should have to chase, and the disagreement you have to handle in front of other people.

    Academic communication

    General academic English for study and research contexts: reading sources critically, reporting them in your own words, and holding your own in a seminar. Not examination coaching.

    Resource centre

    Assessment & review

    A check at the start, a review after each area, and an integrated review at the end.

    Capstone

    The B2 integrated communication project

    One subject, carried through every skill: you research it, summarise what you find, argue a position in writing, present it, and then defend it in discussion. It is the clearest evidence that B2 has been reached — not one exercise done well, but the whole range working together.

    What you are working towards

    Plain-English descriptions of B2 ability, written for learners. They paraphrase the CEFR descriptors rather than quote them.

    • Understand the main ideas of complex texts on both concrete and abstract subjects.
    • Follow extended discussion on familiar and more specialised topics.
    • Interact with enough fluency and spontaneity that conversation is comfortable for both sides.
    • Explain a viewpoint clearly and say what it rests on.
    • Develop an argument with supporting reasons and relevant examples.
    • Weigh advantages against disadvantages and reach a reasoned conclusion.
    • Produce detailed, connected writing on a wide range of subjects.
    • Adjust register and tone to the audience, the purpose and the situation.
    • Take an active part in discussion, accounting for and sustaining your views.
    • Understand a wider range of spoken and written English, including implied meaning.
