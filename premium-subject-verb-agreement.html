Your learning route

Seven steps from diagnosis to certificate

Each step shows its status. You can move freely, but the sequence below gives the most coherent progression.

    Step 1 · Diagnostic

    Find your starting point

    Fifteen quick questions: five from each pathway. Answer them all, then check to see a recommended starting pathway. The diagnostic does not affect your certificate.

    Steps 2–4 · Learning pathways

    Elementary, intermediate and advanced modules

    Each module moves from objectives and core explanation to examples, common errors, guided and independent practice, a summary and a check for understanding.

    Step 5 · Cumulative review

    Bring the pathways together

    Twenty mixed questions and a connected editing passage. Every answer links back to the module that teaches it.

    Extra practice bank · 14 sets on agreement and supporting grammar

    Step 6 · Final assessment

    Fifty questions across four CEFR tiers

    The assessment opens in a focused window. Your progress is saved on this device.

    Step 7 · Certificate

    Certificate of Achievement

    Certificate preparation opens when you submit your first assessment answer. The certificate is issued only after the full assessment is finished.

    For teachers

    References and notes on usage

    Explanations distinguish grammatical rules from meaning-based, regional and editorial choices. The treatment of variation follows these sources:

    • Biber, D., Johansson, S., Leech, G., Conrad, S., & Finegan, E. (1999). Longman grammar of spoken and written English. Longman.
    • Bock, K., & Miller, C. A. (1991). Broken agreement. Cognitive Psychology, 23(1), 45–93. https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(91)90003-7
    • Cambridge Dictionary. (n.d.). Subject–verb agreement. In English grammar today. dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/subject-verb-agreement
    • Cambridge Dictionary. (n.d.). Nouns: singular and plural. In English grammar today. dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/nouns-singular-and-plural
    • Caplan, N. (2022, March 23). My team is winning (or are they?). Cambridge English World of Better Learning. cambridge.org/elt/blog/2022/03/23/my-team-is-winning-or-are-they/
    • Council of Europe. (2020). Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, teaching, assessment — Companion volume. Council of Europe Publishing.
    • Huddleston, R., & Pullum, G. K. (2002). The Cambridge grammar of the English language. Cambridge University Press.
    • Quirk, R., Greenbaum, S., Leech, G., & Svartvik, J. (1985). A comprehensive grammar of the English language. Longman.
    • Swan, M. (2016). Practical English usage (4th ed.). Oxford University Press.

    Scoring. Every assessment question is worth one point. “Select all” questions require the complete correct set; there is no partial credit or negative marking. The course level is the highest consecutive tier with at least 70% in that tier and each earlier tier; below 70% at A2 is reported as “A2 developing”. Tier labels are instructional estimates, not a validated placement scale, and the certificate does not certify overall language proficiency.
