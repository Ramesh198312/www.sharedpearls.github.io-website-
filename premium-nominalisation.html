What is Nominalisation?

It is the grammatical alchemy of transforming verbs (actions) or adjectives (descriptions) into nouns (static concepts). Instead of the basic "The government decided to regulate", academic writers elevate their prose to "The government's decision regarding regulation", creating denser, highly professional phrasing.

Where to deploy it?

  • 1 IELTS Task 1: Critical for describing trends. "Prices fluctuated" (Band 6) becomes "There was a fluctuation in prices" (Band 8+).
  • 2 IELTS Task 2 & Uni: Creates an objective, formal tone by eradicating personal pronouns. ("I think it's difficult" → "The difficulty of...").

The Architecture of Band 9

Examiners scan for three interconnected signals: lexical resource, cohesion, and a rigid academic register.

Nominalisation delivers all three simultaneously. By converting a dynamic clause into a static concept, you create elements that can be counted, qualified, and chained logically.

  • →
    Lexical density: Spoken English is ≈20% nouns; Oxford journals are ≈62%. Nouns carry more information.
  • →
    Objectivity: Removes subjective framing ("people think"). The actor disappears, leaving pure data.

Morphology

The 3 Core Transformations

TYPE 1

Verb → Noun

-tion / -ment / -ance / -al
  • decide → decision
  • evaluate → evaluation
  • develop → development
TYPE 2

Adj → Noun

-ness / -ity / -ance
  • difficult → difficulty
  • stable → stability
  • accurate → accuracy
TYPE 3

Clause → Phrase

The + [Noun] + of
  • Because data was inaccurate
    → The inaccuracy of the data
  • Although policy failed
    → Despite the failure of...
