Sentence Structure

A sophisticated account of how words become constituents, constituents become clauses, and clauses become meaning.

Sentence structure is the study of how linguistic constituents are organised, combined and related to one another — hierarchically, functionally and semantically. It concerns not only what a sentence contains, but how its parts govern, modify and depend on one another to produce grammatical form, propositional meaning and discourse effect.

    04 — Meaning and Use

    Syntax, Semantics and Pragmatics

    Structure does not merely encode grammatical relations; it also governs compositional meaning and shapes how information is packaged for a listener or reader.

    Comparative case study

    Active, canonical order

    The committee rejected the proposal.

    Passive, agent demoted

    The proposal was rejected by the committee.

    The propositional content — that a rejecting event occurred, with the committee as agent and the proposal as patient — remains broadly constant across both sentences. What changes is information structure: the passive promotes the proposal to subject and clause-initial topic position, backgrounds the agent (which could be omitted entirely), and redirects discourse prominence toward the outcome rather than the actor. Word order here is not a stylistic accident; it is a syntactic mechanism for managing what a sentence is "about."

    05 — Hierarchical Composition

    Sentence Architecture

    Linguistic units combine recursively: words project into phrases, phrases combine into clauses, clauses combine into sentences, and sentences cohere into discourse.

    06 — Classification Systems

    Sentence Types

    Sentences may be classified along three independent axes — structural, relational and communicative. These systems overlap in practice but are not interchangeable.

    By structure

    By clause relationship

    By communicative function

    A single sentence is classified once on each axis simultaneously — for instance, a sentence may be structurally complex, relationally subordinating, and functionally interrogative all at once. The axes describe different properties, not competing labels for the same property.
