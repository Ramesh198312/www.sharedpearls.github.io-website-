Teacher resources for Unit 1

Student Centred Learning · 50 minutes

Outcome. Find who, what, where and when. Use present and past simple and the phrase there is or there are. Core story. Green Haven Park opens to visitors

Prepare. Open the core story, expose paragraph numbers and distribute the worksheet. Keep the answer key for feedback. Use the transcript if a UK browser voice is unavailable.

  1. 0–5 minutes. Show the article headline only. Ask learners to predict the decision, event or issue, then collect predictions without judging them.
  2. 5–14 minutes. Read the first two paragraphs silently. Learners mark two details and choose a word whose meaning can be inferred from context.
  3. 14–22 minutes. Partners explain how they inferred a word and check the glossary. Model one comparison between a claim and its supporting paragraph.
  4. 22–35 minutes. Groups choose a second story from the same unit, make a three-point brief and challenge another group to show evidence for each point.
  5. 35–45 minutes. Complete the worksheet individually. Compare one answer with a partner and use the paragraph numbers to resolve differences.
  6. 45–50 minutes. Collect an exit slip with one supported statement and its evidence. Identify a shared point to revisit.

Learner task. Find the main event and two useful details. Use a clear time or place in the final version.

Differentiate. Offer the first two paragraphs and three glossary terms before independent work. Ask confident readers to compare the lead with A free sports morning comes to Bellford.

Assess. Check that factual details point to paragraphs and that claims retain their original certainty. Use the worksheet key for the core questions.

Homework. Read the fourth story and write a new brief using the same evidence check.

Task Based Learning · 50 minutes

Outcome. Find who, what, where and when. Use present and past simple and the phrase there is or there are. Core story. Library adds seats and a free computer class

Prepare. Open the core story, expose paragraph numbers and distribute the worksheet. Keep the answer key for feedback. Use the transcript if a UK browser voice is unavailable.

  1. 0–7 minutes. Set the publication task. Each group is producing a short bulletin for a reader who has not seen the story. Examine headings and identify the intended audience.
  2. 7–15 minutes. Read or listen to the story once for the main idea and again for key details. Compare what the headline leads you to expect with what the text establishes.
  3. 15–30 minutes. Assign a writer, evidence checker and reader. Draft a headline and short bulletin. Mark who said each contested or uncertain claim and cite a paragraph for each firm statement.
  4. 30–40 minutes. Exchange bulletins. The reviewers mark one accurate detail and one sentence that should be clarified, attributed or qualified.
  5. 40–47 minutes. Revise the bulletin and deliver it aloud. Ask the audience to identify the main change and one remaining question.
  6. 47–50 minutes. Collect the worksheet and record whether each group’s headline is supported by its evidence.

Think Pair Share · 50 minutes

Outcome. Find who, what, where and when. Use present and past simple and the phrase there is or there are. Core story. Neighbours clear litter from a riverside path

Prepare. Open the core story, expose paragraph numbers and distribute the worksheet. Keep the answer key for feedback. Use the transcript if a UK browser voice is unavailable.

  1. 0–8 minutes. Read the story independently and record the key change and two details in the worksheet. Allow a second reading or transcript.
  2. 8–15 minutes. Think individually about which detail would matter most to a person affected by the story. Give a reason and note a paragraph number.
  3. 15–27 minutes. In pairs, compare evidence and choose one statement both can support. Identify where a second interpretation is possible.
  4. 27–37 minutes. Compare with another pair. Give the competing accounts a fair hearing and agree which wording best reflects the article.
  5. 37–45 minutes. Share the statement and its source with the class. Peers ask one factual question and one question about interpretation.
  6. 45–50 minutes. Ask learners to revise one sentence and explain the edit on their exit slip.

Learner task. Find the main event and two useful details. Use a clear time or place in the final version.
