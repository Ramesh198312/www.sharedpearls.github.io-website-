01 · Foundations

Keep the meaning. Change the viewpoint.

Reported speech tells someone what another person said, asked or meant, without necessarily repeating their exact words. We use it in messages, meeting notes, news and everyday conversation.

EXACT WORDS“I like this course,” Nina said.

→

A LATER REPORTNina said she liked the course.

Statement: speaker + said + (that) + clause.
With a listener: speaker + told + person + (that) + clause.

That is often optional: “Nina said she liked it” and “Nina said that she liked it” both work. In this pattern, tell needs a listener: told me. Use said to me, not said me.

“Tell a story” and “tell the truth” are different tell-patterns; they do not always name a listener.

A reliable five-step method

  1. Identify the message type. Is the speaker stating, asking, requesting, suggesting or promising?
  2. Choose a reporting verb and pattern. Use said + clause, asked + question clause, or told someone + to-infinitive, for example.
  3. Map the people. Identify the speaker and listener before changing I, you, my or our.
  4. Check the time and location. Decide whether to backshift and whether expressions such as tomorrow or here still fit.
  5. Read for meaning. Check that the report preserves facts, uncertainty, negatives and the order of events.

One transformation explained

At school on Monday, Maya says to Ben: “I will lend you my notes tomorrow.” You report this from home on Wednesday.

Worked transformation
DecisionChangeWhy
Reporting patternMaya told Ben (that) …A statement addressed to Ben; told takes a listener.
PeopleI → she; you → him; my → herThe speaker is Maya and the listener is Ben.
Verbwill lend → would lendUse standard backshift after told.
Timetomorrow → the following day / on TuesdayTomorrow referred to the day after Monday.

Choose the right sentence pattern

Message typeReporting patternDirect speechReported speech

Yes/no questionasked + if/whether + subject + verb“Did you finish?” Ana asked me.Ana asked me whether I had finished.

Quotation marks normally disappear in indirect reports. Keep names capitalised. A statement ends with a full stop; a report inside an outer question can end with a question mark.

02 · Tense changes in reported speech

Direct tense → reported tense

When the reporting verb is in the past, such as said, told or asked, the reported verb often moves into a past form. This is backshift. Identify the complete verb phrase before changing it.

Present simple → past simple: “I work” → she said she worked.
Past simple → past perfect: “I worked” → she said she had worked.

The following table uses standard backshift. Each report is made later by another person. A tense change must preserve the original meaning, not simply replace a word.

Complete tense change table
Direct speech tenseReported speech tense or formDirect speechReported speech

Present continuousPast continuousI am cooking dinner.He said he was cooking dinner.
Present perfect simplePast perfect simpleI have finished the task.She said she had finished the task.
Present perfect continuousPast perfect continuousI have been studying for two hours.He said he had been studying for two hours.
Past simplePast perfect simpleI bought a new bag.She said she had bought a new bag.
Past continuousPast perfect continuousI was waiting for the bus.He said he had been waiting for the bus.
Past perfect simplePast perfect simple — unchangedI had already eaten.She said she had already eaten.
Past perfect continuousPast perfect continuous — unchangedI had been working all morning.He said he had been working all morning.
Future simple with willwould + base verbI will call.She said she would call.
Future continuouswould be + -ingI will be travelling.He said he would be travelling.
Future perfect simplewould have + past participleI will have finished by Friday.She said she would have finished by Friday.
Future perfect continuouswould have been + -ingI will have been teaching for ten years by June.He said he would have been teaching for ten years by June.
am / is / are going towas / were going toI am going to apply.She said she was going to apply.

How to build the changed verb

Simple past: use the past form, including irregular forms: go → went; buy → bought; am/is → was; are → were. Past perfect: use had + past participle: went → had gone; bought → had bought. Past perfect continuous: use had been + -ing.

Keep negatives negative: “I do not know” → she said she did not know. “I did not see it” → he said he had not seen it. Passive voice also stays passive: “The room is being cleaned” → she said the room was being cleaned.

Try it: Report “I have been waiting for an hour,” said Jo.

Model answer: Jo said they had been waiting for an hour. Have been waiting becomes had been waiting; use a pronoun that fits Jo.

Try it: Report “I did not take your pen,” Ben said to Ana.

Model answer: Ben told Ana he had not taken her pen. Did not take becomes had not taken; your refers to Ana.

Modal verbs and semi-modals

Some modals change; others usually remain unchanged. First decide whether the modal expresses ability, permission, obligation, possibility or deduction.

Do not replace must not with did not have to: the first prohibits an action; the second says it was unnecessary. For an obligation that remains current, must can sometimes remain must. The intended meaning and time determine the choice.

Try it: Report a past obligation: “I must submit the form,” said Ria.

Model answer: Ria said she had to submit the form. Compare a deduction: “It must be correct” → Ria said it must be correct.

When backshift is optional or unnecessary

Backshift decisions
SituationRuleExample
Present reporting verbNo backshift is normally triggered by says or tells.“I enjoy reading.” → She says she enjoys reading.
A fact or situation is still trueThe original tense may remain; a backshifted report can also be grammatical.“I live in York.” → Raj said he lives/lived in York.
General truthsThe present form is often retained when the reporter treats the statement as true.“The Earth orbits the Sun.” → The teacher said the Earth orbits the Sun.
Past event with clear timePast simple may remain if the sequence is clear; past perfect makes the earlier timing explicit.“I visited Rome in 2024.” → She said she visited/had visited Rome in 2024.
Past perfect or unchanged modalDo not invent a further past form.“I had left.” → He said he had left. “I should leave.” → He said he should leave.

A backshifted form does not automatically mean the situation has ended. In practice tasks asking for standard backshift, use the table; in real reports, use context.

03 · Pronouns and time adverbials

Keep people, dates and places accurate.

Pronouns and possessives

Identify who spoke, who listened and who is reporting now. There is no universal rule that I always becomes he or that you always becomes me. Change each pronoun to keep the same person in view.

Pronoun and possessive changes
Change in this exampleOriginal words and peopleReported version
I → sheMaya: “I am ready.”Maya said she was ready.
me → herMaya: “Ben helped me.”Maya said Ben had helped her.
my → herMaya: “I lost my key.”Maya said she had lost her key.
mine → hersMaya: “The blue bag is mine.”Maya said the blue bag was hers.
we / our → they / theirThe players: “We have our tickets.”The players said they had their tickets.
you → I, when reporting words addressed to youMaya to me: “You are early.”Maya told me I was early.
you / your → he / his, when the listener is BenMaya to Ben: “You forgot your bag.”Maya told Ben he had forgotten his bag.
I stays I, when reporting your own wordsI said: “I am ready.”I said I was ready.

Reflexive pronouns follow the same logic: “I made it myself,” said Maya → Maya said she had made it herself. Third-person pronouns often remain unchanged, but names are safer when he, she or they would be ambiguous.

Place and demonstratives

Place and viewpoint changes

thisthat, if the object is no longer near the reporterI like this bag.She said she liked that bag.

Context comes first. Today can stay today if it is still the same day. Here can stay here if the reporting location is unchanged. This can stay this if the object is still near the reporter. Exact dates and place names often make a report clearer.

One message reported on two different days

On Monday, Maya says, “I will visit tomorrow.” On Monday you can report: “Maya said she would visit tomorrow.” On Thursday, report: “Maya said she would visit the following day” or “Maya said she would visit on Tuesday.” Do not use Thursday’s tomorrow to refer to Tuesday.

Try it: On Friday, Ben says “I met Ana yesterday.” Report this on Sunday.

Model answer: Ben said he had met Ana the day before/on Thursday. The day before is measured from his original Friday statement.

Try it: At school on Monday, Lia says “I will bring these books here tomorrow.” Report from home on Wednesday.

Model answer: Lia said she would take those books to school the following day/on Tuesday. Naming the place avoids ambiguity; bring/take depends on the reporter’s viewpoint.

04 · Questions

A question in meaning.
A statement in word order.

In a reported question, put the subject before its verb. Remove question-only do/does/did and make the tense change in the main verb instead.

Wh-question → keep the question word

“Where do you live?” → She asked me where I lived.

Yes/no question → if or whether

“Are you ready?” → She asked if I was ready.

Use where I was, not where was I.
Use if I had finished, not had I finished.

An ordinary reported-question statement ends with a full stop. An outer question can still have a question mark: “Did she ask where you lived?” If who is the subject, its position may already be correct: “Who called?” → He asked who had called.

How to transform each question type

Reported question patterns
Question typeOriginal questionReported questionWhat changed
Wh-question with do“Where do you study?” she asked me.She asked me where I studied.Keep where; remove do; study → studied.
Wh-question with did“When did you arrive?” he asked me.He asked me when I had arrived.Remove did; use had + arrived.
Wh-question with be“Why are you waiting?” she asked us.She asked us why we were waiting.Use subject we before were.
Yes/no question“Have you paid?” he asked me.He asked me if/whether I had paid.Add if/whether; keep subject before had.
Negative question“Why don’t you call?” she asked me.She asked me why I did not call.Keep the negative meaning; use statement order.
Subject question“Who broke it?” he asked.He asked who had broken it.Who is already the subject; do not add another subject.
Alternative question“Do you want tea or coffee?” she asked me.She asked whether I wanted tea or coffee.Whether introduces the alternatives naturally.

If or whether? Both introduce ordinary yes/no reports. Use whether before an infinitive (“whether to go”) and immediately before or not (“whether or not I agreed”). “She asked if I agreed or not” is also possible.

Try it: “Why did you leave early?” Kai asked me.

Model answer and reason: Kai asked me why I had left early. The auxiliary did disappears; left becomes had left. The report does not use why had I left.

05 · Requests & instructions

Report the action someone wants.

Use the intention and the situation to choose your reporting verb. A polite question can function as a request.

asked / told / advised / warned + person + to + verb.
For a negative instruction: person + not to + verb.

REQUEST“Please send me the link,” Ana said to me.

→

REPORTAna asked me to send her the link.

INSTRUCTION“Don’t open the door,” the guide said to us.

→

REPORTThe guide told us not to open the door.

“Can you carry this?” may be a request: “She asked me to carry it.” In an ability test, it could be a real question: “She asked whether I could carry it.”

Requests instructions advice and permission
PurposeOriginal wordsNatural report
Polite request“Would you send me the file, please?” she said to me.She asked me to send her the file.
Strong instruction“Leave the building now,” the guard said to us.The guard ordered us to leave the building immediately.
Advice“You should rest,” Jo said to me.Jo advised me to rest.
Reminder“Remember to bring your ticket,” she said to me.She reminded me to bring my ticket.
Warning“Don’t touch the wire,” he said to us.He warned us not to touch the wire.
Request to be allowed to act“May I leave?” I asked.I asked whether I could leave / asked for permission to leave.

A question mark in the original does not decide the reporting pattern. “Could you help me?” can be a request; “Could you swim when you were five?” asks about ability. Keep the purpose clear.

Try it: “Please do not tell anyone,” Ana said to me.

Model answer and reason: Ana asked me not to tell anyone. This is a negative request: asked + me + not to + tell.

06 · Reporting verbs

Choose a verb. Learn its pattern.

Precise verbs explain the speaker’s purpose. They also change the grammar that follows. Do not turn a possibility into a promise or an opinion into a fact.

promise / offer / agree + to

Noor offered to help.
Dan promised to send the file.

deny / admit / suggest + -ing

Kim denied losing the keys.
Tia suggested meeting online.

explain / claim / confirm + that

She explained that the room was full.
He claimed that he knew the answer.

advise / remind + person + to

She advised me to rest.
He reminded us to bring our tickets.

apologise for + -ing

He apologised for arriving late.

suggest + that-clause

She suggested that we should leave.
She suggested that we leave.

Explain something to someone: “She explained the rule to me,” not “She explained me the rule.” Avoid “suggested me to go”; use “suggested going” or “suggested that I should go”.

Reporting verb patterns beyond said
Reporting verbPatternExample
agree / refuse / offer / promiseverb + to-infinitiveHe refused to answer. She offered to help.
admit / denyverb + -ing or that-clauseShe admitted making a mistake. He denied that he had lied.
suggest / recommendverb + -ing or that-clauseHe recommended taking the train. She suggested that we leave.
persuade / encourage / inviteverb + person + to-infinitiveShe encouraged me to apply. He invited us to join him.
warnverb + person + not to; or person + thatShe warned us not to enter. She warned us that the floor was wet.
apologiseverb + to person + for + noun or -ingHe apologised to me for losing the key.
accuseverb + person + of + -ingShe accused him of copying her work.
blameverb + person + for + noun or -ingHe blamed me for missing the bus.
insistverb + on + -ing; or that-clauseShe insisted on paying. He insisted that he was right.

explain / mention / announceverb + (to person) + that-clauseShe explained to me that the office was closed.

Report tone accurately. Claimed presents a statement as someone’s claim; confirmed suggests verification. Promised adds commitment. Admitted suggests accepting something, often reluctantly. Choose these only when the original situation supports that meaning.

07 · Context & nuance

Good reporting is a choice.

Still true: “Raj said he lives in York” emphasises that this remains true. “Raj said he lived in York” is also possible; it does not necessarily mean he has moved.

Present reporting verb: “She says she likes the course.” A present report does not normally trigger backshift by itself.

Deduction or obligation? “It must be the right room” → She said it must be the right room. “I must leave” → She said she had to leave, when reporting a past obligation.

Wishes and exclamations: report their meaning naturally. “Good luck!” → He wished me luck. “What a lovely view!” → She exclaimed that the view was lovely. A rigid word-for-word conversion is not always helpful.

Conditionals and other forms that need care

Meaning before mechanical changes
Original formUsual treatmentExample
First conditionalCan backshift after a past reporting verb.“If it rains, I will stay home.” → She said that if it rained, she would stay home.
Second conditionalUsually keeps its hypothetical past form.“If I had time, I would help.” → He said that if he had time, he would help.
Third conditionalUsually remains unchanged.“If I had known, I would have called.” → She said that if she had known, she would have called.
Wish / unreal pastDo not mechanically backshift an already hypothetical form.“I wish I knew.” → He said he wished he knew.
Used toUsually unchanged.“I used to cycle.” → She said she used to cycle.
Past perfect in a time sequenceKeep the sequence; avoid shifting every subordinate verb mechanically.“I had finished before she arrived.” → He said he had finished before she arrived.

Guided practice before the assessment

Write each report first. Then open the model answer and explain your changes. Use standard backshift unless a question asks you to discuss context.

1. “I study every evening,” said Ana.

Model answer and reason: Ana said she studied every evening. Present simple → past simple.

2. “I am looking for my keys,” said Ben.

Model answer and reason: Ben said he was looking for his keys. Am looking → was looking; my → his.

3. “I have sent the email,” said Cara.

Model answer and reason: Cara said she had sent the email. Present perfect → past perfect.

4. “We played well,” the team said.

Model answer and reason: The team said they had played well. Past simple → past perfect; we → they.

5. “I was working,” said Dan.

Model answer and reason: Dan said he had been working. Past continuous → past perfect continuous.

6. “I will be waiting,” said Eva.

Model answer and reason: Eva said she would be waiting. Will be + -ing → would be + -ing.

7. On Tuesday: “I saw him yesterday,” said Finn. Report on Friday.

Model answer and reason: Finn said he had seen him the day before/on Monday. Yesterday is anchored to Tuesday.

8. On Monday: “I will call tomorrow,” said Grace. Report on Wednesday.

Model answer and reason: Grace said she would call the following day/on Tuesday. Do not make tomorrow refer to Thursday.

9. “Where have you been?” Hari asked me.

Model answer and reason: Hari asked me where I had been. Use subject I before had.

10. “Please wait outside,” Ida said to us.

Model answer and reason: Ida asked us to wait outside. A request uses asked + listener + to-infinitive.

11. “Let’s meet at six,” said Jo.

Model answer and reason: Jo suggested meeting at six / that we should meet at six. Both are valid suggestion patterns.

12. “I live nearby,” said Kai. You know this remains true. Give two reports.

Model answer and reason: Kai said they live nearby / lived nearby. The present highlights current truth; backshift remains possible.

Apply the rules to a real message

Situation: At the library on Monday, Ana tells Ben, “I have booked this room for tomorrow. Can you bring your laptop? Please don’t arrive late.” You report the exchange from home on Wednesday.

Write one connected report before opening the model.

Model answer and reason: Ana told Ben she had booked the library room for the following day/on Tuesday. She asked him to bring his laptop and not to arrive late. The report preserves the completed booking, the request, the negative instruction, the listener and the intended day.

Self-check: Underline your verb changes, circle your pronouns and highlight your time expressions. If you changed a word, explain why. If you kept it, explain why the original form still fits.

A five-point editing checklist

1. Have I preserved the original message?
2. Is it clear who spoke and who listened?
3. Does the tense fit the reporting time?
4. Are time and place references accurate?
5. Does the reporting verb have the right pattern?
